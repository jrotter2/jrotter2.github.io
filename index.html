        02 // HOW I WORK
        
Understand it.
Then build it.

      

      

        

          Physics taught me to understand a system from first principles. Software engineering lets me build it.
          I love the work that needs both: knowing why something behaves the way it does, then writing the
          software that puts that to use. In grad school that meant designing ML models small and fast enough
          to run on FPGA hardware in under 25 nanoseconds. On the side it meant years of full-stack work
          shipping real products.
        

        

          The common thread is the part I love most: taking something vague and hard, getting it into
          people's hands fast, and iterating until it really works. That's what pulled me from physics into
          startups, and it's what keeps pulling me toward founding something of my own.

PL-01ML · HARDWARE

          
Nanosecond ML on FPGAs

          
Led a small team building a boosted decision tree that estimates particle momentum in real time. It had to be compact enough for FPGA hardware and make each decision in under 25 ns.
